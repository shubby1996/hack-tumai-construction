SiteFlow – Platform Explanation

Tum.ai Construction Hack · Challenge B · last updated 20 Nov 2026 (open-source AI) · v0.3

SiteFlow is a shared two-week site plan with an AI assistant on top. Every subcontractor updates its own tasks by voice from site. The assistant flags blockers before they bite (preventive), proposes a re-plan when something breaks (reactive), and drafts every notice and report under VOB/B. The site manager approves re-plans and outgoing documents.

2. The problem it solves

Site managers lose most of their day to paperwork, and money is lost when that paperwork is late or missing. Many subcontractors share one short-term plan, but each changes its part alone, so nobody sees the knock-on effect until a crew stands idle or a milestone slips.

What the challenge owner stressed:

  • Claims get lost. The claims manager (Nachtragsmanager) sits far from the site. Without timely documentation, no claim is filed.
  • Defects are expensive. Rework costs far more than people expect.
  • Delays are found too late. "Suddenly we are two months behind."
  • Stoppages waste crews. When work stops, the crew should do something useful instead.

SiteFlow answers this with one shared plan and two kinds of support. Preventive support clears blockers before work starts. Reactive support re-plans the moment something breaks. Both produce the documentation automatically.

3. How it works

Every input becomes a structured entry in one shared plan and site diary. Two loops run on that plan, and every output waits for the site manager's approval.

The two-week plan itself (tasks, crews, dependencies, readiness checklists) is loaded once and then changed by foremen, stoppages and approved re-plans.

5. Features by screen

The app has four tabs: across the top on desktop, and as a bottom tab bar on phones. A persona switch sits in the header. On desktop the header also has "Open on phone" (a QR code to open the app on a phone) and "Reset demo"; on phones, Reset is at the bottom of the page. The app can be added to the phone home screen.

Today (the main working screen)

  • Capture a site situation: a large record button for voice (German or English), a text box, photo upload and one-click demo phrases.
  • Photo understanding: each photo is described by a small open vision model (caption, visible findings, text in the photo). The description feeds the structured entry, and a photo alone can start a note.
  • Review before saving: the note comes back as a structured entry (type, task, cause, responsible party, start time, expected delay). Missing facts are highlighted and the assistant asks one follow-up question with quick-answer chips.
  • Suggested next steps: after saving, the matching notice is offered (for example an obstruction notice after a stoppage).
  • Re-plan proposal: after a stoppage, the knock-on effect, the milestone slip with and without action, idle crew-hours avoided and each proposed action with its reason. Approve or reject.
  • Blockers to clear: the top 3 open readiness items in the next 5 working days, with "Draft request" and "Mark cleared".
  • Notifications for the current persona, and Today at a glance: preventive vs reactive decisions and milestone status.
  • My tasks (foremen only): own tasks with an editor to shift dates, change duration or status, with a live impact preview.

Plan

  • Two-week Gantt chart (Mon 23 Nov – Fri 4 Dec 2026) grouped by team, with crew size, readiness warnings, milestone markers, delays striped red and blocked tasks outlined. On phones a list view (dates, crew, status, open items) is the default, with the timeline one tap away.
  • Click a task to see readiness details and change it.

Diary

  • Chronological site diary with every field, the original transcript and photos.
  • Decision log tagged preventive, reactive or change.
  • Change log (audit trail): who changed what, old and new values, reason, tasks moved and who was notified.

Documents

  • List of drafts and approved documents, tagged preventive, reactive or report.
  • Editor for drafts; approved documents are locked.
  • Approve (site manager only), export to PDF and Word, and "+ Daily report".

6. The plan engine

The engine is deliberately simple and explainable: a forward pass over the task graph, a rule-based readiness scan and a greedy re-plan. Every proposal states its reason in plain words. Code: src/lib/engine.ts.

6.1 Scheduling

Days are working days (Mon–Fri) in the two-week window. Each task starts on its planned day or the day after its last predecessor finishes, whichever is later. Stoppage delays are added to the task's duration.

ES(t) = max( plannedStart(t), max over predecessors p of EF(p) + 1 )
EF(t) = ES(t) + duration(t) + delay(t) − 1

Milestone slip is the finish of the milestone task minus its target day (never below zero).

6.2 Change impact

Any change (by a foreman, a stoppage or a re-plan) is scheduled before and after. Tasks whose start or finish moves are listed, their teams and the site manager are notified, and any new milestone slip is flagged.

6.3 Preventive scan

Each task has a readiness checklist: predecessor, material, crew, equipment, permit, drawings. Every day the engine looks at tasks starting in the next 5 working days with an open item. Alerts are ranked by due date first, then by slack to the nearest milestone. Only the top 3 are shown, to avoid alert fatigue.

6.4 Reactive re-plan

After a stoppage the engine proposes, in this order:

  1. Recover milestone slip by adding one extra crew to downstream tasks that can be shortened (each task has a minimum duration), within the limit of 1 extra crew per day and no overtime.
  2. Give the stopped crew work for the rest of today: pull forward a task of the same team whose predecessors are finished and whose readiness is fully cleared.
  3. Fill idle days of other teams caused by the delay, inside the 5-day horizon, with the same pull-forward rule.

The objective is fewest days of milestone slip first, then fewest idle crew-hours. Cost is a constraint, not the goal.

Demo result. A 1-day excavation stoppage in section C would slip "Foundation section C complete" by 1 day. The proposal adds a crew to the foundation formwork (2 → 1 day), moves the earthworks crew to backfilling section A for the rest of the day, and brings formwork walls section B forward. All milestones hold and about 72 idle crew-hours are avoided.

7. AI and extraction

SiteFlow uses small open-source models by default. It talks to any OpenAI-compatible chat endpoint (Ollama, Groq, Hugging Face, vLLM, LM Studio, OpenRouter), so models can be swapped through environment variables without code changes. Code: src/lib/ai.ts.

Engine priority: open model (LLM_BASE_URL set) → Claude (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY set) → built-in rule-based extractor. If a model call fails, the app falls back to the rules automatically. The header shows which engine and models are active.

7.1 Models

JobRouteDefault open modelSize (Ollama)

Photo understandingPOST /api/visionqwen3-vl:4b-instruct via VLM_MODEL (smaller: qwen3-vl:2b-instruct)3.3 GB / 1.9 GB

One Qwen3-VL model can handle both jobs, so a single 3.3 GB download is enough for a laptop demo. Qwen3-VL is released under Apache 2.0.

7.2 Note extraction

The note, the speaker, the task list and any photo descriptions are sent with a grounded prompt and JSON mode. The answer is cleaned (thinking tags, code fences and surrounding prose are removed) and checked: unknown entry types become "note", task IDs must exist in the plan, empty fields are dropped.

Entry types: stoppage, delivery, defect, incident, meeting, attendance, progress, instruction, note.

Grounding rules. The model may use only facts stated in the note or visible in the photo descriptions, and may pick a task only from the plan. It never invents times, amounts, parties or causes. Missing required facts are listed and turned into one follow-up question. For a stoppage the required facts are task, cause, responsible party and expected delay.

7.3 Photo understanding

When a photo is added, the vision model returns a one-sentence caption, a suggested entry type, visible findings (for example "crack in concrete wall") and any readable text (labels, delivery notes). The result is shown under the photo, passed into note extraction, stored with the diary entry, and printed in defect and obstruction notices as "Fotobefund (automatisch erkannt, bitte prüfen)". It describes only what is visible and does not guess causes or responsibility.

7.4 Human check

Every extracted field and photo description is shown for correction before saving. Nothing is saved, sent or re-planned without a person confirming.

7.5 Speech-to-text

Speech-to-text runs in the browser (Web Speech API: Chrome, Edge, Safari). It is free and needs no key. Typing works in every browser. An open Whisper model is on the roadmap for offline use.

9. Architecture, tech stack and data model

SiteFlow is a single Next.js app on Vercel. All logic except the AI calls runs in the browser, and state is kept in the browser's local storage, so there is no database to run for the MVP.

Core data objects (src/lib/types.ts)

  • Task: id, name, team, section, planned start, duration, crew, predecessors, milestone, status, delay days, minimum duration, readiness checklist.
  • Readiness item: open or cleared, note, owner, due day.
  • Diary entry: time, day, author, type, transcript, summary, fields (task, cause, responsible party, start time, expected delay, material, quantity, location…), photos, photo descriptions, extraction engine and model.
  • Notice: type, title, addressee, body, references, status (draft / approved), mode (preventive / reactive / report).
  • Change record: who, task, before, after, reason, tasks moved, who was notified.
  • Decision: preventive, reactive or change, with text and time.
  • Notification: recipients, text, kind, read status.
  • Settings: max 1 extra crew per day, max 2 overtime hours per day (not used by the re-plan), 8-hour day, 5-day preventive horizon, 3 alerts.

10. Demo script

The demo is one simulated day, Tuesday 24 Nov 2026, and takes about 5 minutes. Press "Reset demo" first.

  1. 07:00, preventive. As Max Bauer (site manager), open Today. "Blockers to clear" shows three items: crane double-booked Thursday, slab drawings missing from the client, steel for section C unconfirmed. Click Draft request on the drawings, review it in Documents and Approve.
  2. 10:00, reactive. Click the demo phrase Excavator idle (DE), then Structure note. Answer the follow-up with 1 day and Save to site diary.
  3. Re-plan. The proposal shows a 1-day slip on the foundation milestone without action, "milestones hold" with the re-plan, and about 72 idle crew-hours avoided. Approve re-plan.
  4. Notice. Click Draft obstruction notice, edit a sentence, Approve, then PDF and Word.
  5. 11:00, change control. Switch to Lena Weber (formwork). Under My tasks, open "Formwork slab floor 2 A", press +1 day, enter a reason and Apply change & notify. Switch to Mehmet Kaya (rebar): he has a notification that his task moved and that the floor 2 milestone now slips 1 day.
  6. 17:00, report. Switch back to the site manager, open Documents, click + Daily report and export it.

In one line: one shared two-week plan for the whole site. The assistant clears blockers before they bite, re-plans in seconds when they do, and writes every notice and report along the way. The site manager just approves.

11. Deployment and operations

The app runs on Vercel (Hobby plan, project hack-tumai-construction) and redeploys automatically on every push to main. The first build took 37 seconds.

Environment variables

Set them in .env.local for local runs, or in Vercel → Project → Settings → Environment Variables (then redeploy). See .env.example.

VariableRequiredEffect
LLM_BASE_URLNoOpenAI-compatible endpoint of the open model, e.g. http://localhost:11434/v1 (Ollama) or https://api.groq.com/openai/v1. Turns on open-model extraction and photo understanding.
LLM_MODELNoModel for notes (default qwen3-vl:4b-instruct)
VLM_MODELNoModel for photos (default: same as LLM_MODEL)
LLM_API_KEYFor hosted endpointsAPI key of the hosted provider (any value for Ollama)
ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, ANTHROPIC_MODELNoOptional paid alternative, used only when LLM_BASE_URL is not set

Running the open models

  • Local, fully offline: install Ollama (0.12.7 or newer), run ollama pull qwen3-vl:4b-instruct, copy .env.example to .env.local and run npm run dev. The app and the model must run on the same machine.
  • On Vercel: Vercel cannot reach a model on your laptop. Point LLM_BASE_URL at a hosted open-weight endpoint. The live app uses the Groq free tier: LLM_BASE_URL=https://api.groq.com/openai/v1, LLM_MODEL=llama-3.1-8b-instant (text), VLM_MODEL=qwen/qwen3.8-27b (photos, preview model) and LLM_API_KEY from console.groq.com. Check Groq's model list if a preview model is retired.

Run locally

npm install
npm run dev          # http://localhost:3000
npm run check:engine # prints the engine's results for the demo scenario

Operational notes

  • Voice input needs HTTPS, which Vercel provides.
  • State lives in each browser's localStorage. Different devices do not share data, and clearing browser data or pressing "Reset demo" restores the starting state.
  • Only the note text, the speaker's name and role, the task list and photo descriptions are sent to the note model; photos are sent only to the vision model. With local Ollama nothing leaves the machine. Documents stay in the browser.

12. Limitations and roadmap

Current limitations

  • No shared database or login: all personas share one browser, so each foreman cannot yet use their own phone.
  • Notifications appear in the app only (no push or email).
  • Voice input depends on the browser's speech recognition and does not work in Firefox.
  • Small open models (2–4B) make more extraction mistakes than large ones; the review step and the rule-based fallback cover this. The open-model path was tested against a stand-in server, not yet against a real Qwen3-VL instance.
  • The plan is a fixed synthetic two-week plan (14 tasks); there is no import from Excel or MS Project yet.
  • The re-plan heuristic does not model crew capacity limits per team, so a pulled-forward task can overlap another task of the same team.
  • Legal references in the templates have not been reviewed by a construction lawyer.
